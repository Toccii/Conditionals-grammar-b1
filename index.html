Zero Conditional
Facts & General Truths
Use it for: scientific facts, habits, things that are always true
Structure If + Present Simple  →  Present Simple
  • If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.Sempre vero – legge della fisica
  • If it rains, the ground gets wet.Conseguenza naturale
  • If I drink coffee at night, I can't sleep.Abitudine personale
💡 Tip: You can replace "if" with "when" and the meaning stays the same.
When you heat water to 100°C, it boils. ✓
1
First Conditional
Real & Likely Future
Use it for: real possibilities, warnings, promises, plans
Structure If + Present Simple  →  will + infinitive
  • If it rains tomorrow, I will stay at home.È possibile che piova → conseguenza reale
  • If you study hard, you will pass the exam.Promessa / incoraggiamento
  • If she calls, I will tell her the news.Piano per il futuro
⚠️ Watch out: After "if" we use Present Simple, NOT "will".
❌ If it will rain…    ✅ If it rains…
2
Second Conditional
Unreal / Hypothetical Present
Use it for: imaginary situations, wishes, advice (were/was)
Structure If + Past Simple  →  would + infinitive
  • If I had a million euros, I would travel the world.Non ho un milione di euro – è immaginario
  • If she lived closer, we would see each other more.Non vive vicino – situazione contraria alla realtà
  • If I were you, I would apologise.Consiglio con "if I were you"
💡 Tip: With the verb "to be", we use "were" for all persons in formal/correct English:
If I were rich… / If she were here…
(In spoken English, "was" is also common.)
3
Third Conditional
Unreal Past / Regrets
Use it for: past situations that didn't happen, regrets, criticism
Structure If + Past Perfect  →  would have + past participle
  • If I had studied more, I would have passed the exam.Non ho studiato → non ho passato – rimpianto
  • If she had left earlier, she wouldn't have missed the train.Non è partita prima → ha perso il treno
  • If they had known, they would have helped.Non lo sapevano → non hanno aiutato
⚠️ Remember: Both verbs refer to the past. The situation is impossible to change now.
"Would have" is often contracted: I'd have passed / She'd have missed
